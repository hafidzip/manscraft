## 4.9 Range and cost accounting

**Range.** `uFarFogEnd = (5 + 0.75)·16·0.92 = 84.6 m`, `VIEW_DISTANCE = 5` → 80 m of
meshed chunks. Far cascade covers 96 m and dissolves 72 → 88 m, i.e. it ends inside
opaque fog. Effective stable shadow range **32 m → 88 m = 2.75×** ✅ criterion 1.
Raising it is one line (`SHADOW_FAR_HALF_EXTENT`) *if* `VIEW_DISTANCE` ever grows.

> **Frustum geometry worth knowing.** With the static sun at elevation 0.22 (12.7°) the
> ortho box's local-Y axis is only 12.7° off world-up, so a half-extent of *H* metres buys
> ≈ *H* metres of ground radius **across** the sun azimuth and far more **along** it —
> bounded by the depth range, which is why `CELESTIAL_SHADOW_FAR` goes 180 → 260.
> Sizing by "half-extent = radius" is therefore conservative, never optimistic.

**Cost**, in units of one 2048² depth pass, per refresh:

| | today | after |
|---|---|---|
| Near, day | 2.00 (sun **+** moon, both always rendered — `castShadow` alone decides) | **1.00** (sun only) |
| Near, twilight (~40 s / 300 s) | 2.00 | 1.25 (2048 sun + 1024 moon) |
| Near, night | 2.00 | 0.25 (moon 1024 only) |
| Far cascade | — | 0.25 per render, ~1 render per 8 m walked ≈ **0.05** amortised |
| Grass casters (Task 3) | 0 | +0.4…0.5 on the near pass only |
| **Typical daytime total** | **2.00** | **≈ 1.05** |

Net **≈ 0.53× today**, and ≈ 0.75× worst case in twilight — comfortably inside the ≤1.6×
budget ✅ criterion 3, with the headroom spent on grass. Two structural savings made this
possible: (a) `shadow.autoUpdate = false` per light so zero-intensity lights stop
re-rendering, (b) the elevation trigger from 2.4 is gone.

**Blend correctness.** The far term multiplies `reflectedLight.direct*` *after*
`<lights_fragment_begin>`, so it is conjunctive with three's near cascade rather than
selective: in the 18–32 m overlap a penumbra edge can darken slightly more than either
cascade alone. Fully-lit (1×1) and fully-shadowed (0×0) are exact; only PCF edges differ,
and the blend completes at 28 m — inside the near box's 32 m inscribed circle — so there
is no gap and no hard seam ✅. A selective split would require replacing three's
`getShadow` call site, which is a much larger blast radius for an invisible gain.

**Residual limitation, stated plainly:** during twilight both lights are lit, and the far
cascade attenuates the *summed* direct term using only the key light's map. Worst case is
a ~40 s window per 300 s day where far shadows are attributed to one light too many. The
honest fix is a second far map; it is not worth 0.25 units for that window.

# TASK 3 — Grass blades cast stable shadows

## 3.0 Design — four independent sources of instability, four fixes

| # | Instability | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Blades billboard toward **`uGrassCam`** in the *depth* material → every shadow silhouette reshapes as the player walks | Billboard toward the **constant light azimuth** instead. Same functional form, but the target angle is a session constant, so each blade has exactly **one** shadow silhouette forever. |
| S2 | Sway is driven by `uGrassTime`, but the map re-renders on demand → shadows twitch at refresh cadence | **Freeze** sway at `t = 0`: `sin(aSway.x)` is still a per-blade random lean, just time-invariant. (The brief's "cheaper + more stable" option.) |
| S3 | The distance-fade **collapse** (`gP.y -= …(1 - gCol)`) is camera-relative, so blades rise/sink in the shadow map continuously | Replace with a dissolve around **`GRASS_SHADOW_ANCHOR`**, frozen at the exact moment the map is baked. |
| S4 | The light frustum tracks the camera every frame with no texel snapping → whole-scene swim | Task 4's `snapShadowLight` + "only move the frustum on the frames you re-render it". |

S4 is why Task 4 merges first. S1–S3 are the same principle three times: **nothing the
shadow pass reads may vary with the view.** After these, the only inputs to the cutout
depth pass are per-vertex constants and uniforms that change solely on the frames the map
is rebuilt.
